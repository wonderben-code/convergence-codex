# Where the constructions meet

Each row is an object, or a structure, that both programmes reach. The two grade columns are
each side's **own** grade, as published. The last column says what the coincidence does for both:
when two derivations that share no premises arrive at the same object, each supports the other.

## The structural rhyme

Beyond individual objects, the two programmes share a *shape*:

- **One sentence at the bottom.** Chain Theory: "Nothing exists but finitely many maximally
  entangled pairs of qudits …". The Tree: "a single minimal algebra, forced by self-reference,
  grows by one repeated operation."
- **Finitude as the key constraint.** Chain Theory from the Bekenstein bound ("look instead for
  finitely many degrees of freedom"); the Tree from minimality (the smallest non-commutative
  algebra, and finite-dimensional levels all the way up).
- **The number 6.** Chain Theory's read cap is d₁d₂ ≤ 6, so the readable world is the qubit pair
  (4) and the qutrit–qubit pair (6). The Tree's spacetime is M₄ and its colour-plus-weak object
  after 4 → 3⊕1 is the 3 ⊗ 2 = 6-dimensional block of M₁₆. The same two dimensions, 4 and 6,
  mark the "visible" sector on both sides.
- **A tree of dependency, not of time.** Chain Theory draws "the derivation graph of the
  compendium re-rooted at the axiom and the three registers" (Figure 1). The Tree *is* a
  dependency tree, and its book's interlude *A Note on Time* makes the same point: the order is
  one of *because*, not of *before*.
- **Three of something at the top.** Chain Theory's three registers (2, 3, 4); the Tree's three
  lineages (End, Aut, ⟨·,·⟩). These are not the same three — but both programmes found that one
  sentence fans out into exactly three strands before it reaches physics.
